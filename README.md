# AI Companion for Research Proposals

Created by Dr Àngels Trias i Valls for PER701 Masters Perspectives, International Relations, Regent's University London, 2026.

## What the Companion is

The AI Companion for Research Proposals is a guided set of web pages that helps students use AI while they prepare a research proposal. It is built so that AI asks the student questions and the student does the thinking. It is not designed to write the proposal for the student. The aim is to help students develop academic judgement, which means the ability to evaluate ideas, question assumptions and make decisions that they can justify.

The Companion is a single HTML file. It runs in any web browser, needs no installation and needs no account. Answers stay in the browser on the student's own device and are not shared with anyone.

## What is inside

**Introduction**

- Front page
- Where are you with AI? A short reflection on which tasks you currently use AI for and who does the work.
- What makes a research proposal. The four Ws (what, where, how and why), the parts of a proposal, the features of a good proposal, writing style, and a table of the types of sentence to look for in example proposals.
- Working with AI to develop academic judgement. For each decision, you can delegate to AI, collaborate with AI, or keep the task for yourself.
- AI tools available for research. What different tools are best for.
- Who is doing the thinking when working with AI? An activity that separates evaluative tasks (where your judgement is needed) from generative tasks (where AI can produce material).

**Practice pages**

Each page follows the same four steps: open the recommended AI tools for that stage, copy a prompt and paste it into the tool, read the response critically, and decide what you will use. Every page has example prompts ("You can ask...") and cautions ("Watch...").

1. Topic
2. Research gap
3. Literature
4. Research questions
5. Contribution
6. Methods
7. Ethics
8. Your positioning (includes the wheel of power, described below)
9. Title
10. Abstract
11. Mock reviewer

**Your AI log.** A record of what you asked AI, what it produced, what you kept, what you rejected and why. At the end you can copy all of your answers into one block of text and paste it into a document.

**AI Disclosure Statement.** Explains how the Companion was developed with the assistance of Claude Sonnet 5.5, and the author's role in deciding its purpose, structure and teaching approach.

## How to use it

1. Download or clone this repository.
2. Open `AI-Companion-for-Research-Proposals.html` in a web browser.
3. Keep `wheel-of-power.html` in the same folder as the Companion. Page 8 links to it, and the link works only when both files are together (or hosted together).
4. Copy your answers into a document before you close the page. Your browser may not keep them.

## The wheel of power, adapted for researchers

Page 8 of the Companion, "Your positioning", asks you to think about your own position in relation to your research. It uses a shorter version of the wheel of power, with nine dimensions chosen for researchers. The wheel is based on the idea of intersectionality, which was developed by Kimberlé Crenshaw and describes how different parts of a person's identity and circumstances combine, so that a person may be closer to the centre in some respects and closer to the edge in others.

**This is not a test, and there are no right or wrong answers.** Nobody else sees your answers. For each dimension, select the option that best describes you. You may leave any dimension blank, and several options are available so that you can choose "prefer not to say".

How to read the wheel:

- The **centre** describes positions that are more likely to align with dominant social norms, which often means more social power and fewer barriers.
- The **periphery** describes positions that are more likely to be marginalised, which often means more barriers and less recognition.
- The map shows one line for each dimension, so you can see where you may be close to the centre and where you may be close to the edge.
- A position changes with place and time. The same person may be at the centre in one country and at the edge in another.

| Dimension | Options you can choose from |
| --- | --- |
| **Nationality and citizenship** | I hold a nationality. I have dual or triple citizenship. I am undocumented. My nationality is not recognised. |
| **Language** | I speak English or Spanish. I speak the official language of my country. I speak a minority language. I cannot write the language that I speak. I speak a different language from my parents. |
| **Education** | University. Master's. PhD. Secondary school only. No formal education. Public schools. Private schools. |
| **Class background** | I had financial security. I had limited financial means. I was the first in my family in higher education. My financial situation changed. |
| **Gender** | Male. Female. Non-binary. Prefer not to say. |
| **Sexuality** | Heterosexual. LGBTQIA+. Prefer not to say. Not sure. |
| **Neurodiversity** | Neurotypical. Neurodivergent. Not sure. Prefer not to say. |
| **Rural or urban setting** | Urban. Rural. Both. |
| **Relationship to the people I will research** | I belong to the community. I am an outsider. I am partly an insider and partly an outsider. I hold authority over the participants. I depend on the participants for access. |

There is also a free-text box for **other aspects of my positioning** that the list does not cover.

After you have chosen your options, the page invites you to reflect on how your position may shape the questions you ask, the people you can reach, what participants may tell you, and how you interpret what you find. Many of these reflections also feed into the ethics section of your proposal.

### The full wheel of power

If you wish to try a more complex and more intersectionally represented version, open `wheel-of-power.html`. It has 16 dimensions (gender, sexuality, marital status, race, skin colour, indigeneity, citizenship, language, geography, wealth, housing, education, neurodiversity, health, ability and body size), four example profiles to explore, and dots that you place on the wheel or set with sliders. It runs privately in your browser tab. Its scoring was designed for this activity and is not a published method. The interpretation draws on Hancock (2007), Bowleg (2008) and Bauer (2014).
